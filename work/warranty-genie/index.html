Warranty Genie

A warranty-claims platform where AI reads invoices and photos and flags problems, without ever pretending to decide who is entitled to a warranty.

Overview

  • Warranty teams matched invoices, inspected photos and chased missing evidence by hand, mostly on routine cases.
  • Built a claims platform with Claude-on-Bedrock evidence reads, a rules engine and a triage board that routes only exceptions to people.
  • On a 100-claim sample batch, triage routed 11 claims to a person; Claude read 20 sample workshop photos blind in 79 s.

Problem

Warranty teams check claims by hand: matching invoices to products, inspecting photos, chasing customers for missing evidence and spending hours on cases that turn out to be routine. An uploaded file proves nothing, and an LLM can't certify that a purchase is genuine either. The system needed AI that reads evidence and flags problems without pretending to decide entitlement.

Solution

Warranty Genie has separate customer and reviewer workspaces. Customers submit purchase details and private evidence; a background worker runs warranty rule checks and Claude-on-Bedrock invoice and photo reads, and a claim assistant handles clarification until a person takes over. A claims review board triages batches of dealer claims with rules, history signals and blind photo reads, so only exceptions reach a person. It runs as one Next.js app, one worker, PostgreSQL and Caddy under Docker Compose.

Architecture

  1. A customer submits product and purchase details with an invoice and photos; the app checks file bytes and stores evidence privately.
  2. The worker claims the assessment job with FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED and receives a five-minute lease and fencing token.
  3. Rules compute the warranty window; Claude on Bedrock reads the invoice and photos into schema-checked findings per file.
  4. Wrong-product or unrelated evidence returns the claim to the customer; consistent evidence goes to a reviewer.
  5. A chat assistant answers until handoff; a generation counter discards stale replies after a human takes over.
  6. The reviewer weighs AI findings against the original evidence and records an immutable decision with a reason.

Key decisions

PostgreSQL as the job queue, with leases and fencing

No Redis or SQS. A worker claims a job with FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED and gets a five-minute lease; every completion must match the lease token issued at claim time, so a stalled worker can't overwrite a job another worker took over. Transient errors get three attempts with backoff, refusals fail fast, and a graceful shutdown re-queues without burning an attempt.

Bounded, structured evidence reads

Claude reads invoices and photos through Bedrock with native PDF and image input and a JSON-schema output, validated with Zod, and every finding is tied to an exact evidence ID. Each assessment is capped at six files or 20 MB with skipped files listed for manual review, and uploaded content and customer messages are always treated as data, never instructions.

AI advises; people decide

There are no genuineness percentages or fraud scores, because an LLM cannot certify a convincing fake. Wrong-product invoices or unrelated images send the claim back with a specific request; uncertain evidence goes to a reviewer. Overriding a flag requires a written note, and final decisions cannot be changed through the API.

Blind photo reads and a pure triage function

In dealer-claim triage the vision call never sees the claimed part, price or dealer, so the photo can contradict the claim on its own. Reused photos are caught with SHA-256 plus perceptual-hash distance alongside VIN check-digit and repeat-repair checks. Lane routing is a pure function shared by server and browser, so guardrail sliders re-route a batch instantly.
