Forward Pricing Intelligence

Blends contract and market prices into forward prices and buy decisions, and rejects any assistant answer whose figures don't trace to the data.

Overview

  • A food manufacturer's buyer priced future quarters by hand in a spreadsheet, from data sources that disagreed with each other.
  • Built deterministic blend and BUY/REVIEW/HOLD engines, plus a Bedrock pricing assistant whose every number is checked against what it actually read.
  • 13 materials × 6 quarters priced with the arithmetic in words; 600+ tests and 70 graded eval cases for the assistant.

Problem

A food manufacturer's buyer priced future quarters by hand, blending booked SAP contract volume with market prices against a demand plan in a spreadsheet. The data didn't agree with itself: the plan's booked quantities and SAP's undrawn balances disagreed in three different ways across 13 materials, and several prices had no licensed feed at all.

Solution

A zero-dependency Node.js app gives every material and quarter a blended forward price, with the arithmetic written out in words so a buyer can check it by hand. A deterministic rules engine turns coverage gaps into BUY, REVIEW or HOLD. A Bedrock-backed pricing assistant explains results through read-only lookups, and a numeric-grounding checker rejects any answer containing a figure that doesn't trace to data the assistant actually read. Missing prices prompt a person and are never guessed.

Architecture

  1. Importers parse SAP exports and client workbooks, dry-run every refresh and never overwrite human-edited cells.
  2. A daily timer pulls public commodity prices; exchange closes are refetched once they are 12 hours stale.
  3. The blend engine caps coverage at 100%, then prices contract × coverage plus market × remainder per quarter.
  4. The rules engine issues BUY, REVIEW or HOLD per material, never netting plan bookings against SAP balances.
  5. A buyer asks the assistant; Bedrock picks read-only lookups, and every returned figure enters a per-question ledger.
  6. The checker traces each number to the ledger for its named material and quarter; any miss shows the deterministic answer.

Key decisions

Code decides, the model explains

Deterministic code computes blended prices and BUY/REVIEW/HOLD instructions from stored data, and that route makes no external calls. The assistant only picks lookups and words the answer; it can never produce a price, place an order, contact a supplier or forecast. Refusal rules run on the question before any model call.

Grounding by rounding bands, not equality

The checker treats each number the model writes as the band of values that would round to it, so "$0.83" is a true statement about 0.8337 and "$0.84" is not. A number is compared only with ledger entries for the material and quarter its sentence names. One failed figure rejects the whole answer, with no partial repair; the built-in answer is shown and the reason logged.

Carry conflicting sources, never net them

The plan's booked quantities and SAP's undrawn balances disagree in three different ways. Subtracting one from the other would have shown one material as fully covered when it was materially short, so the engine keeps both figures and names the discrepancy. Coverage is capped, because a quarter booked at 150% would otherwise produce a discount that doesn't exist.

Zero-dependency, reversible operations

The app runs on Node's built-in http, sqlite and test modules with no third-party runtime packages. A custom XLSX reader parses macro-enabled client workbooks without ever running a macro. Each release is tested on the server and switched live, reverting automatically if the health check fails, and every edit is audited with before and after values.
